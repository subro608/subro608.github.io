About

Research Engineer, NYU Neuroinformatics Lab · M.S. Computer Science (Neuroinformatics), NYU Tandon, 2026

I am a full-time Research Engineer in the NYU Neuroinformatics Lab, working with Prof. Erdem Varol. I recently graduated with an M.S. in Computer Science (Neuroinformatics) from the NYU Tandon School of Engineering.

I build tools and analyze large-scale neural data spanning brain–computer interfaces (BCIs), Neuropixels electrophysiology, and related multimodal recordings, with collaborators including Prof. Saurabh Vyas and Prof. Attila Losonczy. My first-author paper, LFPformer, was accepted at NeurIPS 2026.

Longer term, I want to help build brain implants and neural interfaces that let people control and communicate with AI agents and the Internet as fluidly as imagined in Neuromancer and Cyberpunk 2077: technologies in the spirit of neural lace, direct brain–computer links, and closed-loop neuroprosthetics.

Before joining the lab full-time, I worked as a Computer Vision Engineer at Bigvision.ai, building end-to-end computer vision systems for retail and e-commerce. I earned a B.E. (Honours) in Electrical Engineering from Jadavpur University, where I published work in medical imaging and computer vision. During my master’s, I also served as a Course Assistant at NYU Stern (Consulting Capstone, Corporate Finance, and Foundations of Corporate Finance, Summer 2025), and developed a CRE loan-financing workflow with multi-LLM agents under Prof. Anthony Marciano.

Research

My research is in neuroinformatics: foundation models for neural data, multimodal translation across electrophysiology, imaging and transcriptomics, representational alignment between the brain and large world models, and tooling for BCIs and Neuropixels-scale recordings.

  • LFPformer

    NeurIPS 2026 · first author

    Self-supervised LFP representations that use only the probe geometry and insertion coordinates logged at acquisition, for 3D localization and brain-region decoding across labs, species and probes.

  • LFP2Gene

    NeurIPS 2026 Workshop · Foundation Models for the Brain and Body

    Decoding the local gene-expression profile near each recording site from pretrained LFPformer features, matched to spatial transcriptomics through a common atlas.

    • OpenReview
    • NeurIPS

  • In vivo ↔ ex vivo cell registration

    bioRxiv 2026 · under review

    Linking two-photon calcium imaging in behaving mice to spatial transcriptomics of the same hippocampal interneurons, with a 4-modality 3D viewer.
